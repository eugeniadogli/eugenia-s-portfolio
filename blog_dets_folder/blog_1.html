On Composition
and Coding

A few thoughts on giving ideas room to breathe, whether they live on a page or on a screen.

Structure / Space / Story

01 — Starting with the idea

Make space for what matters.

Good composition starts before the details. I like to first notice what should catch the eye, what can support it, and where a little breathing room will help the whole piece feel clearer.

On a drawing, that can mean balancing shapes and empty space. On a website, it can mean deciding what belongs together and how someone will move from one part to the next.

02 — The visual side

Composition gives the eye a path.

Contrast, scale, and alignment all help guide attention. When everything asks to be noticed at once, it is difficult to know where to look. A clear focal point gives the rest of the work a place to gather around.

“

A little structure can make room for more creativity.

03 — Bringing it to the web

Code is part of the composition.

Building a page asks many of the same questions: what should stand out, how much space should things have, and will the arrangement still make sense on a smaller screen? HTML gives the content its structure, while CSS shapes its rhythm, color, and spacing.

I am learning that the best results come from treating design and code as partners. Each small decision adds to how the final idea feels and how easily someone can experience it.
